Job Market Paper

Exploring the Differential Impact of Repealing the ACA Individual Mandate by Health Status

Abstract
This paper examines how the 2019 elimination of the Affordable Care Act (ACA) individual mandate penalty affected insurance outcomes by health status. Using nationally representative CPS ASEC data, I exploit cross-state variation in mandate enforcement by comparing states without enforceable mandates with Massachusetts, New Jersey, and the District of Columbia, which had enforceable mandates in 2019. The policy increased the probability of becoming newly uninsured, with a statistically significant increase among relatively healthy consumers but not among relatively sick consumers. The benchmark Marketplace premium for a 27-year-old increased by $739, while reported out-of-pocket premium spending declined by $284.60. Financial-incidence analysis suggests that accounting for ACA subsidies attenuates the financial burden that would otherwise fall more heavily on relatively sick consumers. The findings show that the incidence of adverse selection depends not only on Marketplace premiums but also on how public subsidies shape consumers’ exposure to premium increases.

Working Papers

Does the Tobacco 21 Minimum Legal Sales Age Policy Improve Respiratory Health? Evidence from the United States

With M.O. Emmanuel, G. Aipoh, V.J. Dickson, and E.N. Collins

Revise and Resubmit, Social Science & Medicine

Abstract
In this paper, we examine whether Tobacco 21 minimum legal sales age laws reduce smoking and improve respiratory health among individuals aged 18–20. Using BRFSS data from 2011–2019 and variation in policy adoption across states, we find that Tobacco 21 laws significantly reduce smoking but do not lead to a statistically significant decline in asthma prevalence in the short run. These findings suggest that reductions in smoking may not immediately translate into measurable improvements in respiratory health.
