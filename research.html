Exploring the Differential Impact of Repealing the ACA Individual Mandate by Health Status

This paper examines how repeal of the Affordable Care Act individual mandate affected consumers differently by health status. Using nationally representative CPS ASEC data and a two-way fixed effects framework, I find that the repeal reduced insurance coverage, with the decline concentrated among relatively healthy consumers.

Although lower coverage is associated with higher Marketplace premiums, consumers' out-of-pocket premium payments declined. The findings show how premium subsidies and other incentives can affect the distribution of welfare losses.

Does the Tobacco 21 Minimum Legal Sales Age Policy Improve Respiratory Health? Evidence from the United States

(with M.O. Emmanuel, G. Aipoh, V.J. Dickson, E.N. Collins). Revise and Resubmit, Social Science & Medicine

In this paper we examine whether Tobacco 21 minimum legal sales age laws reduce smoking and improve respiratory health among individuals aged 18–20. Using BRFSS data from 2011–2019 and variation in policy adoption across states, we find that Tobacco 21 laws significantly reduce smoking but do not lead to a statistically significant decline in asthma prevalence in the short run. These findings suggest that reductions in smoking may not immediately translate into measurable improvements in respiratory health.

Working Paper

Prescription Drug Insurance, Functional Health, and Informal Care

I study whether the benefits of prescription drug insurance extend beyond the insured individual by reducing the need for informal care. Using Health and Retirement Study data and a difference-in-differences estimator that accounts for heterogeneous treatment effects, I examine SSDI Medicare beneficiaries and family caregiving.

The findings suggest that improved access to prescription medications enhances functional health and reduces the need for care from family members.
